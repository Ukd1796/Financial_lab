Top 5 Architectural Bottlenecks

🔴 Bottleneck 1 — Exclusive Ownership Lock-out Breakout dominates BUY execution due to highest universe hit-rate. Once Breakout owns a position, TrendPB and RSI-MR SELL signals are silently dropped for that symbol (dropped_ownership counter). Documented ~3,600 blocked SELL signals per period in allow_cross_strategy_exits=False mode. Other strategies cannot exit a losing Breakout position until the ATR stop fires — by which time capital is already committed.
🔴 Bottleneck 2 — Universe Activity Bias Starves 3 of 5 Strategies Scoring formula 0.40×rank(rel_vol) + 0.30×rank(|return|) + 0.30×rank(vol_5d) ranks by activity. DualMA (needs quiet golden-cross stocks), TrendPB (needs low-volume pullbacks), and RSI-MR (needs oversold-in-uptrend) candidates are structurally under-represented in the top-80. The per-strategy filters operate on an already-biased input pool.

Update 2026-05-24 — tested and ruled out as the source of the universal entry-quality defects. A 6-factor quality-weighted rebalance (0.20 rel_vol + 0.10 abs_daily_return + 0.20 rolling_vol_5d + 0.25 trend_persistence + 0.15 trend_intact + 0.10 pullback_quality) was implemented in app/universe/dynamic_agent.py and replayed across the six periods. The three universal metrics — MFE efficiency (still −0.12 to −0.30), persistence half-life (still 0.0 days), false breakout rate (max −1.4pp move) — did not budge. Regime composition did shift (Bull 2019 improved +2.5pp return, Bear 2022 regressed −3.3pp return as trend-quality factors over-selected uptrending stocks during a bear market). Change rolled back. The activity bias is real and worth fixing, but it is a downstream amplifier of Bottleneck 6, not the root cause of the entry-quality numbers.
🟡 Bottleneck 3 — Capital Fragmentation at 5-Strategy Equal Weight At weight=0.20: risk_budget = equity × 0.005 × 0.20 = 0.1% per trade. Max position = equity × 0.20 × 0.20 = 4%. Low-frequency strategies (DualMA fires once per golden cross; TrendPB needs pullback alignment) rarely fill their slots — their capital allocation sits unused in cash.
🟡 Bottleneck 4 — RSI-MR Structurally Near-Disabled _REGIME_WEIGHT_BOUNDS caps RSI-MR at ≤0.05 in BEAR_CONFIRMED, BEAR_EARLY, CRASH, RECOVERY, BULL_LOWVOL, BULL_SUSTAINED, BULL_MEDVOL. The only regime without an explicit cap is MIXED (≤0.10). RSI-MR's backtested Sharpe is negative in Mixed (-0.14) and Bear/Choppy (-0.65) — so it is correctly capped, but the cap makes it an observer rather than a contributor.
🟡 Bottleneck 5 — 2-Week Regime Stability Lag regime_stability_weeks=2 means a new regime must appear for 2 consecutive weekly rebalances before weights shift. In fast-moving markets (e.g. COVID crash, post-election rally) the system can hold inappropriate weights for 2–3 weeks. Combined with the 5-day rebalance frequency, effective lag is 10–15 trading days.
🟡 Bottleneck 6 — Same-Bar Breakout Entry (HYPOTHESIS FALSIFIED 2026-05-24) BreakoutMomentumStrategy.decide() and QuietBreakoutStrategy.decide() both fire BUY on the same bar that closes above their N-day high with only a vol_ratio > 1.2 filter. This was initially hypothesised as the source of the universal opportunity-quality defects (persistence ½-life = 0, MFE eff < 0, false breakout 27-37%) and tested via a two-bar confirmation + candle-quality + tighter-volume intervention on both strategies. The intervention produced a system-wide regression of ≈ −50pp cumulative return across the six periods — five of six periods regressed, average loss-per-trade worsened on the surviving trades, and Bear 2022 flipped from +10.24% to −1.31%. Both strategies have been reverted. See §12 Finding 6 for the full post-mortem. The universal metrics appear to be descriptive of momentum systems running through difficult regimes, not addressable strategy-layer defects.

The router runs all active strategies against the same shared portfolio state and produces at most one Decision per symbol per day.

2.2 Dispatch Modes — 2-param vs 3-param

The router detects dispatch mode once at construction using inspect.signature. This is a critical architectural distinction because it determines how many symbols each strategy processes per call.

DualMovingAverageStrategydecide(market_state, portfolio)Per-symbol (2-param)Called once per symbol in a loop. Cannot compare across symbols or issue batch decisions.

QuietBreakoutStrategydecide(current_date, symbol_states, portfolio)Multi-symbol (3-param)Same as Breakout but 20-day lookback.
TrendPullbackStrategydecide(current_date, symbol_states, portfolio)Multi-symbol (3-param)Maintains internal _entry_dates dict for hold-day tracking.

ℹ️ DualMA Architectural Consequence DualMA's 2-param signature means it processes each symbol in a tight loop inside _dispatch(). It cannot see the portfolio's full state while deciding — each call is isolated. This means DualMA cannot avoid allocating to a symbol that another strategy already has a pending BUY on (conflict resolved later in merge).

2.3 Conflict Resolution Logic

All decisions land in merged: dict[str, tuple]. The _merge_into() method applies a 2-level priority system:

# From app/strategy/multi_router.py
_ACTION_PRIORITY = {"SELL": 2, "BUY": 1, "HOLD": 0}

def _merge_into(self, merged, d, w, name):
    p_new = _ACTION_PRIORITY.get(d.action, 0)
    if sym not in merged:
        merged[sym] = (d, w, name)          # first decision always wins
        return
    existing_d, existing_w, existing_name = merged[sym]
    p_old = _ACTION_PRIORITY.get(existing_d.action, 0)
    if p_new > p_old or (p_new == p_old and w > existing_w):
        # New wins → bump dropped_priority for the displaced strategy
        self.diag_counters[existing_name]["dropped_priority"] += 1
        merged[sym] = (d, w, name)
    else:
        self.diag_counters[name]["dropped_priority"] += 1
ScenarioWinnerRule
Breakout=SELL vs DualMA=BUY on RELIANCEBreakout SELLPriority: SELL(2) > BUY(1)
Breakout=BUY (w=0.30) vs DualMA=BUY (w=0.25)Breakout BUYSame priority, higher weight wins
TrendPB=BUY vs RSI-MR=BUY on HDFCBANKTrendPB BUY (if w higher)Same priority, weight tie-break
Breakout=HOLD vs QuietBrk=BUYQuietBrk BUYPriority: BUY(1) > HOLD(0)

⚠️ Critical: HOLD from ATR stop path When a strategy emits HOLD (position still intact, no exit signal), RiskAgent still evaluates it and may convert it to SELL via the ATR trailing stop. This is intentional — the HOLD is required precisely to trigger the ATR stop check. Strategies that skip emitting HOLD for held positions cause the ATR stop to silently never fire.

3.1 Exact Scoring Formula

opportunity_score = 0.40 × percentile_rank(relative_volume) + 0.30 × percentile_rank(|daily_return|) + 0.30 × percentile_rank(rolling_vol_5d)

All three inputs are cross-sectionally rank-normalised to [0,1] across all symbols with valid data on the current date. No raw values reach the scoring function — only percentile ranks.

# app/universe/dynamic_agent.py — select_candidates()
for col in ["relative_volume", "abs_daily_return", "rolling_vol_5d"]:
    scores[f"{col}_rank"] = scores[col].rank(pct=True)    # 0→worst, 1→best

scores["opportunity_score"] = (
    0.40 * scores["relative_volume_rank"]
  + 0.30 * scores["abs_daily_return_rank"]
  + 0.30 * scores["rolling_vol_5d_rank"]
)
top = scores["opportunity_score"].nlargest(self.top_n)

3.3 Momentum Bias Diagnostics

🔴 Structural Momentum Bias — All 3 scoring inputs
  • relative_volume rank (40% weight): rewards volume spikes — momentum precursor
  • |abs_daily_return| rank (30% weight): rewards price magnitude — momentum confirmation
  • rolling_vol_5d rank (30% weight): rewards recent activity — momentum persistence
Stable low-volatility compounders (low rel_vol, low abs_return, low vol_5d) score ≈ 0.0 every day and never enter the top-80. Mean-reversion and pullback candidates are systematically excluded unless they experienced a recent sell-off large enough to boost their vol metrics.

5.2 Ensemble Intelligence Impact

The ownership model was designed to prevent premature exits (e.g. RSI-MR selling a trending Breakout position because RSI crossed 80 mid-rally). This is architecturally sound for clean 1-strategy scenarios. In a 5-strategy ensemble it has unintended side effects:

ImpactMechanismSeverity
Cross-strategy exit intelligence wastedTrendPB recognises a trend-break in a Breakout position; its SELL is droppedHigh
RSI overbought exit blockedRSI-MR issues SELL when RSI_3 > 70 on a Breakout-owned position near peakHigh
Ensemble concentrationBreakout wins most BUYs → owns most positions → all exit signals are Breakout exits → system behaves like a single-strategy BreakoutCritical
Ensemble diversity preserved on entryAny strategy can BUY (no ownership on entry); diversity on BUY is maintainedMitigated

5.3 Alternative Architecture Concepts (Analysis Only)

Concept A — Shared Ownership (conviction voting)

Remove exclusive ownership. Any strategy may SELL any held position. Conflict resolution remains SELL > BUY > HOLD (an exit from any strategy beats a hold from the owner). Opens the door for cross-strategy exit confirmation: if 2+ strategies agree the position should be exited, it exits faster. Risk: high-frequency strategies (RSI-MR 5-day hold) may prematurely exit long-duration Breakout trends.

Existing flag: allow_cross_strategy_exits=True already implements this — it's a one-line configuration change. A/B test noted in code comments.

Concept B — Conviction Aggregation (vote weighting)

Rather than first-past-the-post conflict resolution, accumulate conviction scores per symbol. A SELL from 3 strategies scores higher than a BUY from 1. The highest-conviction action wins, scaled by the sum of agreeing strategy weights. This is architecturally incompatible with the current Decision dataclass (no multi-source field) and would require a new merge layer.

Concept C — Probabilistic Voting

Each strategy emits a probability vector [P(SELL), P(BUY), P(HOLD)]. The router multiplies by strategy weight and sums across strategies. The action with the highest weighted probability wins. This requires strategies to output calibrated probabilities rather than hard decisions — a significant interface change.

Cost Model

BUY exec_price = market_price × (1 + slippage_pct) × (1 + commission_pct) SELL exec_price = market_price × (1 − slippage_pct) × (1 − commission_pct) Default: slippage=0.05%, commission=0.10% per side Round-trip cost: ≈ 0.30% of trade value

Costs are baked into exec_price so that portfolio.cash, position.average_price, and trade_pnl are all automatically post-cost. No separate cost ledger required.

The Zerodha cost_model path adds a flat per-scrip DP charge on SELL (₹5.5 for equity delivery) deducted explicitly from portfolio.cash and portfolio.realized_pnl — this is the only cost not foldable into the per-share price.

Execution Bandwidth Dominance

In BULL_SUSTAINED regime (typical period):

  • Breakout claims ~60–70% of executed trades by count (high universe hit-rate + cash priority)
  • QuietBrk claims ~20–25% (second largest filter output)
  • TrendPB claims ~5–10% (PullbackFilter output small; ownership blocks exits)
  • DualMA claims ~3–5% (few golden crosses per day)
  • RSI-MR claims <2% (weight too low for meaningful sizing on most stocks)

Router Entropy

In an ideal 5-strategy ensemble with equal execution, router entropy would be log₂(5)≈2.32 bits. In practice:

  • Breakout dominates ~65% of execution → entropy ≈ 1.4 bits (significantly below ideal)
  • The ensemble is effectively a Breakout-primary system with occasional QuietBrk overlap
  • DualMA, TrendPB, RSI-MR contribute <10% combined

Signal Collision Rate

On a typical day with 25 active symbols and 5 strategies, expected collision rate:

  • Breakout + QuietBrk: ~30% symbol overlap (both activity-based, different lookbacks)
  • Breakout + TrendPB: ~5% overlap (adversarial filters)
  • All 5 strategies on same symbol: rare (<1% of symbols per day)
  • Per-symbol conflict resolution runs ~10–15 times per day (2–3 per overlapping symbol pair)

§12 Architectural Notes

Note — These are observational findings, not implementation directives The following notes describe what the code reveals about system behaviour. They are intended to frame future experiments, not to prescribe implementation.

Finding 1 — Ensemble collapse to single-strategy behaviour

The combination of (a) Breakout's activity-universe advantage, (b) cash-priority sorting by weight, and (c) exclusive ownership means the portfolio behaves predominantly as a single Breakout strategy. The other four strategies contribute at the margin. The ensemble's theoretical diversification benefit is significantly eroded by these three compounding factors.

Finding 2 — Universe construction predetermines strategy participation

The activity-scored top-80 universe is the first and most impactful filter. Three of five strategies (DualMA, TrendPB, RSI-MR) want candidates that rank poorly in an activity-scored universe. Fixing the universe construction is likely higher-leverage than adjusting weights or conflict resolution rules.

Finding 3 — The ownership model is appropriate for 1-strategy, wrong for N-strategy

The guard comment in multi_router.py acknowledges this: "RSI-MR selling a DualMA position because RSI crossed 80 mid-trend" is the motivation. But in a Breakout-dominant portfolio, the ownership rule mainly blocks exit signals that would reduce risk on losing momentum trades. The ATR stop is the backstop — but it trails from the high-watermark and can give back significant gains before triggering.

Finding 4 — AdaptiveSelector's 2-week regime stability gate introduces a deliberate lag

This is a designed feature (regime whipsaw prevention), not a bug. Its cost is holding suboptimal weights during fast transitions. The 5-day rebalance interval plus 2-week confirmation means effective lag of 10–15 trading days in changing regimes. During RECOVERY phase (which can be <20 days), the system may not fully shift to recovery allocation before the phase ends.

Finding 5 — ATR stop interacts with ownership in an important way

The ATR stop fires in RiskAgent.evaluate() before the ownership check — because the ownership gate runs in the router, and the ATR stop check runs in the risk agent after. Any strategy's HOLD decision reaching the risk agent will trigger the ATR stop check if the position is breaching its stop. This means the ATR stop is the only cross-strategy exit mechanism in the system — it effectively works around the ownership constraint.

Finding 6 — The universal entry-quality defects live in the strategy layer, not the universe (added 2026-05-24)

A 6-factor quality-weighted rebalance of the universe scoring formula (Bottleneck 2) was implemented and replayed across the six backtest periods. Three structural metrics — MFE efficiency (still −0.12 to −0.30), persistence half-life (still 0.0 days in every period), false-breakout rate (max −1.4pp move) — did not budge. The same pattern is observed in both EqualWeight and Adaptive+RCA modes, ruling out the universe layer as the source of these specific metrics. The universe rebalance did have a measurable side effect: regime composition shifted toward HIGH_VOL_UPTREND in bull regimes (helped Bull 2019 by +2.5pp return) and toward LOW_VOL_UPTREND in bear regimes (hurt Bear 2022 by −3.3pp return). Any future universe-layer work should be regime-conditional rather than apply a single static weight set. Change was rolled back after the replay.

Finding 8 — The system is regime-bound; the remaining lever is capital-preservation, not redistribution (added 2026-05-24)

Three failed iterations (universe rebalance, strategy-layer filtering, meta-layer rolling-WR override) all confirm the same underlying constraint: the alternative strategies in the ensemble cannot substitute for Breakout in regimes where Breakout fails. Redistributing weight away from Breakout does not produce profitable trades elsewhere because the signal isn't there to capture.

The realistic engineering responses going forward, in order of complexity:
